# Fixtures

Everything about patients here is synthetic. Policy data is real public record, quoted from each hospital's own published documents through the Waive atlas (CC BY 4.0).

- `case-synthetic.json` — the demo case: Dorothy M. Hale (synthetic), her daughter Jenna, a bill from the synthetic demo partner "Riverbend Community Hospital", the matching Medicare Summary Notice, the $240.00 line-3 discrepancy, income and the synthetic 75% assistance tier, the simulated response, and the five edge cases.
- `hospitals.json` — the three pilot hospitals (Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, CA; Alexian Brothers Medical Center, IL; NYU Langone Hospitals, NY) with their published assistance thresholds, tiers, application windows and contacts, each value with its verbatim quote and dated source, plus the 2026 and 2025 HHS poverty guidelines and the state transition that makes the tool timely (with confidence notes).
- `atlas/ma.json` — the Massachusetts atlas export (32 hospitals). The other eight state exports live in the private `waive-data` checkout; point `ATLAS_DIR` at `…/waive-data/data/atlas` to load them locally.
- `cases/` and `documents/` — created in Phase 2 of `docs/BUILD.md`.

## Private mode (Snap Incognito)

All of it is written by `node scripts/make-fixture-images.mjs` from `documents/html/`, except the HTML pages and `demo-policy.json`, which are edited by hand.

- `documents/html/*-covered.html`, `*-partly.html`, `bill-main-amount-covered.html` and their PNGs — the main bill, notice and income letter as a user might drop them. Covered: the name and the account number under solid black boxes. Partly: the name under a marker stroke, the account number showing. Amount covered: the amount owed under a box, for the hard stop. The synthetic pages print no address and no Medicare number, so there is none to cover. A covered value is deleted from the HTML and a block stands in its place; nothing is hidden with CSS.
- The three demo sets are the uncovered originals (`bill-main.png`, `msn-main.png`, `income-letter.png`), the `-covered` three and the `-partly` three. `Use the demo files` sends the covered set. The originals keep their bytes: the regular extractor knows them by hash through `documents/index.json`, which lists none of the private files.
- `private/reads.json` — what the private demo reader answers, keyed by file base name: `{ "sha256": …, "read": … }` for the ten PNGs. A read is raw, as a reader returns it: the `PrivateRead` keys without `page`, amounts as printed (`"$466.00"`), dates, service codes and the hospital's name. `boundRead` turns it into a `PrivateRead`. A covered or partly covered page has the same read as its original; the amount-covered bill has no amount owed and lists `patient_owes` as covered. No name, address or account number is in the file, and a test asserts it.
- `private/demo-policy.json` — what the demo partner's synthetic policy adds for private mode: its city and state, and one invented sentence saying it covers patients who have insurance.
- `documents/bill-main-exif.jpg` — the synthetic bill as a JPEG with an EXIF block holding invented GPS coordinates (open ocean), for the test that the phone's redraw drops a file's hidden details. Never replace it with a real photo: the repository is public.
- `documents/bill-main-boxed.pdf` (from `documents/html/bill-main-boxed.html`) — the synthetic bill as a one-page PDF where the name and the account number are live text with a black box drawn over each, as a PDF editor's rectangle tool leaves them. This is the opposite of the covered PNGs on purpose: the words are in the file, and `tests/fixtures.test.ts` reads them out of it. It is for the test that the phone turns a PDF's pages into pictures, so the words under the boxes never leave it (`tests/e2e/private-pdf.spec.ts`). It must stay one page: the stub reader takes a second page for the notice.
- `documents/four-pages.pdf` (from `documents/html/four-pages.html`) — four nearly empty pages, one more than a PDF may have, for the refusal on the phone.
- Chrome writes the time into a PDF, so the two PDFs get new bytes on every run of the script while every other file comes out the same. Nothing keys on their hashes; commit them again only when their HTML changed.
